Reading:

- A brief that restates the applicable rules (`rules-only`) matched or beat the installed brain on every grader, on both models and both tasks, at 2.5 to 3 times fewer input tokens. Adding the rules to the brain brief (`embedded`) cost less than the pointer brief and was never worse.
- The brain's value over the bare ticket on Opus is real but narrow: test-first (3/3 vs 0/3) and the contract/consumer handling on task 2 (3/3 vs 1/3). The rules-only brief delivers both.
- On Sonnet the brain arm was the worst arm: one run imported `catalogue.persistence` directly after reading the persona file, and all three made `post_title` required without the web change because the persona forbids web edits; the rules-only brief on the same model made the field optional in 3/3. Three runs is a floor; the direction agrees with the literature on weaker models and instruction overhead (see the review).
- Opus respected ADR-0008 in every arm, including with no brain and no rule in the brief, because the ADR is two directories away in a 12-file repository. The slop violations happened in a 600-PR monorepo where the ADR was not in front of the agent; the fixture does not reproduce that distance. A harder fixture (ADR under `docs/decisions/` among 20 others, client in another package) is the next step before trusting `boundary_ok` as a discriminator.
